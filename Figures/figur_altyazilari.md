# Draft figure captions

All estimates are survey-weighted. n values are unweighted. Data: Turkish Statistical Institute (TurkStat), Income and Living Conditions Survey (ILCS) cross-sectional micro data.

## Figure 1
Study design. (a) Sample flow: the ILCS 2023–2025 cross-sections (239,985 persons in 76,693 households); the 2023 and 2024 cross-sections were used only for context analyses. From the 2025 cross-section (25,836 households, 80,206 persons), one household without an adult member was excluded (household analytic sample, n = 25,835; 2,030 households reporting a heavy medical or medicine expenditure burden). Of the 80,206 persons, 18,281 aged <15 years and 113 aged ≥15 years without an individual interview were excluded (61,812 persons with an individual questionnaire), and a further 3,913 aged 15–17 years (age at 31 December of the income reference year), giving the adult analytic sample (n = 57,899 in 25,835 households). (b) Analytic framework: exposure (household cannot afford a meal with meat, chicken or fish every second day), modifier (adult with chronic illness in the household), outcomes, adjustment set, estimands standardized by g-computation, and the five analysis blocks.

## Figure 2
Food affordability and dietary frequency. (a) Share of persons (all ages) living in households unable to afford a meal with meat, chicken or fish every second day, 2023–2025, overall and by equivalized income quintile, with 95% confidence intervals (bands; household-cluster linearization; weight FK050); each survey year estimated separately. The dashed line shows the EU-27 value (9.5%, 8.5% and 8.5% in 2023, 2024 and 2025; Eurostat, dataset ilc_mdes03 "Inability to afford a meal with meat, chicken, fish (or vegetarian equivalent) every second day", total population, last updated 20 October 2026, accessed 25 October 2026 via the Eurostat dissemination API); the national values reproduce the Eurostat series for Türkiye (39.1%, 39.3%, 35.1%). The 2023 survey has no households from TR63 (Hatay, Kahramanmaraş, Osmaniye). (b) Distribution of consumption frequency among adults aged ≥18 years (2025, weight FB030) by household food affordability; numbers in segments are weighted percentages ≥8%; segments sum to 100%. (c) Standardized prevalence differences (percentage points, 95% bootstrap CI, 4,000 household-level replicates) in low-frequency consumption between adults in households that cannot and can afford the meal, overall and within equivalized income quintiles, from survey-weighted logistic models with affordability interacted with all covariates (age spline, sex, education, income quintile, region, household size, number of children).


## Figure 3
Latent class profiles of food affordability and consumption frequency among adults aged ≥18 years (n = 57,899; weighted by FB030). (a) Item-response probabilities of the four-class model estimated with a joint affordability × meat, chicken or fish indicator plus fruit and vegetable frequency; household food affordability and meat, chicken or fish frequency are shown as marginal probabilities derived from the joint item, and meat frequency has three categories (less than once a week or never; weekly but not daily, i.e., 1–6 times a week; daily); class labels give weighted class shares. (b) Weighted class shares overall and by equivalized income quintile. (c) Weighted class shares by NUTS-1 region. Numbers in segments are weighted percentages ≥7%.

## Figure 4
Household burden of medical or medicine expenditure by food affordability and chronic illness. (a) Standardized prevalence (95% bootstrap CI, 4,000 household-level replicates) of households reporting that medical care or medicine expenditure in the past 12 months was a heavy burden, by food affordability and presence of an adult with chronic illness (n = 25,835 households; weight HB040), from the main model (survey-weighted main-effects logistic regression with core and need covariates, g-computation). The open diamond, placed to the right of the observed estimate for the group with both characteristics, marks the prevalence expected under additivity (p10 + p01 − p00). (b) The same contrast at the adult level (n = 57,899; weight FB030; household clusters), with the adult's own chronic illness as modifier. (c) Share of adults living in a household with a heavy medical or medicine expenditure burden by latent class (three-step BCH estimates, 95% household bootstrap CI, 500 replicates): circles, adjusted for income quintile, age group, sex, education, region and household type; diamonds, additionally adjusted for household food affordability. (d) Additive interaction Δ (percentage points, 95% CI) along the adjustment ladder (minimal; core, all sociodemographic covariates without the need indicator; core + need, main model; fully adjusted, adding disability and bad self-rated health; flexible), alternative model forms, and sensitivity analyses of the main model (for the activity-limitation modifier, the number of adults with activity limitation is removed from the covariates; the second of these rows adds the number of adults with chronic illness); adult-level ladder at the bottom. The dotted line marks the main estimate.

## Figure 5
Plasmode simulation on the 2025 household sample (true covariates, exposure, modifier and weights; outcome generated on the additive scale). (a) Rejection rate (two-sided 5%) of tests of the additive interaction Δ (g-computation with main-effects logistic models, the main estimator for Hypothesis 1, or flexible logistic models; linear probability model product term) and of the logistic product-term test, by true Δ; horizontal lines at 5% and 80%. Covariates: core and need set of the main household model. (b) Bias of the main-effects g-computation estimator of Δ without response bias, with additive false-positive reporting among households that cannot afford the meal (δ = 0.02) and with proportional over-reporting (γ = 0.3). (c) Coverage of 95% confidence intervals (Monte Carlo 95% intervals as bars); squares: percentile bootstrap intervals for the main estimator (200 data sets, 200 bootstrap replicates each). (d) Adult-level analysis with household dependence induced through a latent intraclass correlation of 0.3 or 0.6: ratio of the mean model standard error to the empirical standard error with household clustering (circles) and ignoring it (squares), with the reference line at 1.0; numbers in the upper row give the coverage of the corresponding 95% intervals. 2,000 replicates for Δ = 0 and 1,000 otherwise.
